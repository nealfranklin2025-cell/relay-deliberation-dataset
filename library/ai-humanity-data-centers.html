AI, Humanity, and Data Centers

Author: Clifton O'Neal Franklin
Study completed: September 29, 2026 · Published: October 4, 2026
License: CC-BY-4.0 · Open Access

DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.23138314 (zenodo.org/records/23138314)

Abstract

"What is the future of AI and humanity — are data centers going to be the downfall for both of us?" A seven-AI relay investigation: seven AI researchers each took a lane — capability trajectory, data center physicals, money and geopolitics, jobs and society, environment and communities, the downfall thesis (steelman, then stress-test), and a dissenter — in fresh memory-free chats. Four seats then formed a blind cross-grading panel, grading the other seats' factual claims without knowing whose they were (CONFIRMED, PLAUSIBLE, UNVERIFIABLE, or CONTRADICTED). Every disputed claim was independently adjudicated against primary sources. Public sources only; no logins; no paid APIs. Final adjudicated report, September 29, 2026.

Key findings

  • The scale is real but modest globally. The IEA puts data center electricity at ~415 TWh in 2024 (~1.5% of world demand), rising toward ~945 TWh by 2030. Evidence: adjudicated against primary sources in 10.5281/zenodo.23138314.
  • The verdict: the electrons will be fine, the neighbors might not be. Grid-scale energy supply is not the binding constraint; local impacts — land, water, prices, community pushback — are where the friction concentrates. Evidence: the final adjudicated report, 10.5281/zenodo.23138314.
  • The downfall thesis was steelmanned, then stress-tested. A dedicated seat built the strongest version of the "data centers will be the downfall" case before the panel and adjudicator tested it. Evidence: 10.5281/zenodo.23138314.
  • A dissenter was seated by design. One seat's job was to say where the consensus was most likely wrong — adversarial by construction, not by accident. Evidence: 10.5281/zenodo.23138314.

Note: numerical claims above are as adjudicated in the study; consult the full report for primary-source citations and the blind-grading dispositions.
