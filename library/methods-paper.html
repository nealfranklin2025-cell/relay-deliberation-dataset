Human-Mediated Cross-Model Deliberation: A Jury Protocol for AI-Assisted Research

Author: Clifton O'Neal Franklin
Published: October 3, 2026
License: CC-BY-4.0 · Open Access

DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.23127408

Abstract

We describe a protocol for structured multi-model inquiry — human-mediated cross-model deliberation — and release, to our knowledge, the first public dataset of multi-model deliberation transcripts with claim-level provenance: verbatim jury transcripts, a consolidated verdict, and take/reject lists from a formal deliberation round in which four independent commercial AI systems were consulted separately on the same research question, with a human operator and a coordinator agent ferrying all material between them and no direct AI-to-AI contact. The protocol's components (multi-agent debate, AI juries, human-in-the-loop orchestration) are individually precedented; the contribution is the assembly as a persistent, documented institution, and the dataset as a public research artifact. We report the method in full, summarize the first completed round, document the limitations honestly — including the observer effects that publication itself introduces — and specify the measurement discipline (frozen first answers, claim-level provenance, pre-registered protocols) adopted for subsequent rounds.

Key claims

  • A reusable jury protocol. Each "seat" is one commercial AI chat service in its own native session; seats never communicate directly. A human operator and coordinator agent ferry all material between sessions by hand. Evidence: the protocol is reported in full in the paper, 10.5281/zenodo.23127408.
  • Memory-free jury rounds. Each seat receives the same docket brief in a fresh session and produces an independent verdict before seeing any other seat's work; only after all independent submissions are frozen does the coordinator circulate positions and challenges, recording what changed and why. Evidence: 10.5281/zenodo.23127408.
  • Honest originality accounting. A companion adversarial assessment scored the protocol's mechanics 3 out of 10 for originality ("original operation, unoriginal mechanics"); the paper states the claim advanced is modest — a documented, reusable protocol and a public artifact, not a proof of superiority. Evidence: 10.5281/zenodo.23127408.
  • Companion dataset. The first completed round's transcripts and dispositions are released as a public dataset: Human-Mediated Cross-Model Deliberation: Jury Transcripts and Take/Reject Lists (10.5281/zenodo.23112329).
